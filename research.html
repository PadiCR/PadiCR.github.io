Research

My research follows water through mountain catchments: how rainfall infiltrates, is stored in soils and fractured bedrock, leaves the basin as discharge, and becomes slope failures, debris flows and sediment disasters. The processes that decide whether a slope fails are often invisible from above, so I combine field observation, monitoring, modeling and remote sensing.

Topic 01

Hillslope hydrology and bedrock groundwater

Long-term monitoring of rainfall, groundwater and runoff on steep hillslopes with fractured bedrock, to understand how deep water storage controls runoff generation and deep-seated landslides.

Bedrock GroundwaterRunoff GenerationDeep-seated LandslidesField Monitoring

Representative papers: Padilla et al. (2015), Hydrological Processes; Padilla et al. (2014), Geomorphology.

Topic 02

Rainfall triggers of debris flows and landslides

How rainfall intensity and duration trigger debris flows and slope failures, and how triggering thresholds can support early warning.

Rainfall ThresholdsDebris FlowsEarly Warning

Representative paper: Sepulveda & Padilla (2008), Natural Hazards.

Topic 03

Sediment cascades in mountain river networks

Where debris-flow sediment travels and is stored once it reaches the river network, combining field and drone surveys, grain-size recognition and network sediment-connectivity modeling.

Sediment ConnectivityD-CASCADEDrone SurveysGrain-size Analysis

Representative paper: Padilla, Lelli & Corsini (2026), Geomorphology.

Topic 04

Geospatial tools and data-driven hazard mapping

Landslide inventories and susceptibility mapping from LiDAR terrain models and remote sensing, machine learning with spatial and temporal validation, and open-source tools that make these methods usable by practitioners and government agencies.

LiDARSusceptibility MappingMachine LearningQGIS Plugins

Software: SZR+, SZR+ for ArcGIS and DCASCADE_Qpy.

Topic 05

Current interests: reading the hidden part from the surface

Many variables that control rain-induced disasters lie underground, but they leave signatures at the surface. I am interested in using remote sensing, discharge records and geospatial AI to infer soil moisture, subsurface water storage and past landslides, with implications for slope stability, flood timing and drought. I also follow atmospheric rivers and intense rainfall as hazard drivers, and permafrost thaw in relation to deep-seated landslides.
