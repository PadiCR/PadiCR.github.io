About

I am an engineering geologist and GIS specialist. My work has been driven by two questions: how water moves through mountain landscapes, at the surface and underground, and how it turns intense rainfall into slope failures, debris flows and sediment disasters.

I began with the rainfall triggers of debris flows in the Andes, studied bedrock groundwater and deep-seated landslides in Japan (PhD, University of Tsukuba; Kyoto University), followed the sediment downstream into river networks in Italy (University of Modena and Reggio Emilia), and applied this knowledge to hazard mapping for government users in Central America, South Asia and Japan. I combine field monitoring, physics-based modeling and remote sensing, and I develop open-source GIS tools.

I am now interested in how geospatial AI can read the hidden, subsurface part of these processes from what can be seen at the surface.
