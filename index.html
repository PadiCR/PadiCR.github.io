About

I am an engineering geologist and GIS specialist with more than fifteen years of experience across academia and consultancy. My work centres on landslide hazard, sediment dynamics and the geospatial tools that make natural hazard information usable for disaster risk reduction.

Most recently I was a researcher at the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, where I applied the D-CASCADE sediment connectivity model to debris-flow-driven sediment routing in a mountain river network. Before that I worked at Kyoto University on landslide and slope-hazard research, and in consultancy on disaster risk projects in Central America, South Asia and Japan.

I hold a PhD in Earth Sciences from the University of Tsukuba and a second-level Master in GIScience and UAS from the University of Padua. I work primarily in English and Spanish, with operational communication in Japanese.

I am particularly interested in permafrost thaw and its relationship with deep-seated landslides, and in how atmospheric rivers and intense rainfall drive rain-induced disasters.

Open-source software

  • SZR+, a QGIS plugin for machine-learning landslide susceptibility mapping. I extended the original vector-analysis code with raster-analysis capability and built a simplified interface that also works for teaching.
  • SZR+ for ArcGIS, the same landslide susceptibility approach as an ArcGIS Pro Python Toolbox, for users who work in ArcGIS rather than QGIS.
  • DCASCADE_Qpy, a QGIS interface for the DCASCADE-py sediment cascade model. I built the plugin and its QGIS-native tools, and added stored-sediment outputs. The modelling core is DCASCADE-py by M. Tangi et al.
